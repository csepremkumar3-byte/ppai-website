Author Guidelines

The Indian Journal of Plant Protection (IJPP) is a peer-reviewed quarterly scientific journal published by the Plant Protection Association of India (PPAI) since 1972. It publishes original, high-quality applied research across entomology, plant pathology, nematology, weed science, rodent control, and pesticide application technology.

Indexed By: ICAR e-Publications National Platform Frequency: Quarterly (4 Issues / Year) Review Type: Double-Blind Peer Review Submission: Online Portal / Email

1. Scope and Membership Mandate

Publication in the Indian Journal of Plant Protection is open to members of the Plant Protection Association of India. All authors are required to ensure active membership of the society at the time of publication.

  • Original Applied Research: Manuscripts must report original contributions in applied aspects of plant protection, including insect pests, plant diseases, nematodes, weeds, rodents, and application equipment.
  • Multi-Season Trials Preferred: Routine laboratory bioassays or single-season trials are strongly discouraged. Field studies on integrated pest management (IPM), crop loss assessment, and host plant resistance should ideally be conducted over a minimum of two experimental seasons.
  • Student Thesis Submissions: Research manuscripts originating from Master’s or Ph.D. thesis work must be routed through the student’s research guide or a member of the Journal’s Advisory Board.
  • Processing Charges: Nominal article processing charges apply for all accepted manuscripts as per society norms.

2. Manuscript Preparation and Structure

Manuscripts must be written in clear, concise English and typed in double space on standard A-4 size paper with 2.5 cm margins on all sides. The total manuscript length should not exceed 6,000 words, including tables, figures, and references.

Typography & Formatting Specifications

  • Title & Headings: The paper title (14 pt) and all major section headings (12 pt) must be set in Arial Black.
  • Body Text: The text of all sections should be set in Times New Roman (10 pt), double-spaced.
  • Heading Hierarchy: Primary headings (e.g., Materials and Methods) in 12 pt Arial Black; secondary headings in 10 pt bold; tertiary headings in 10 pt italic running directly into the paragraph.

Core Manuscript Sections

  • Title Page: A concise, informative title with initial capitals for each principal word. Full names of authors, official affiliations, and email addresses. A running title of not more than 50 characters must be provided if the main title exceeds one line.
  • Abstract: A self-contained, structured summary of 200–250 words stating the research problem, methodology, key findings, and practical conclusions. Avoid abbreviations and reference citations in the abstract.
  • Keywords: 4 to 6 relevant keywords listed in alphabetical order immediately following the abstract.
  • Introduction: A brief description outlining the importance of the study, relevant background literature, the specific research gap addressed, and objectives of the investigation.
  • Materials and Methods: Clear and detailed description of experimental design, field location, climate data, sampling procedures, biological materials, treatments, and analytical protocols so that experiments can be replicated.
  • Results and Discussion: Results and discussion should be integrated to avoid repetition. Findings must be supported by empirical data presented in tables or figures and interpreted with reference to relevant published literature.

3. Tables, Figures and Scientific Standards

Data presentation must adhere to rigorous scientific standards to ensure clarity, accuracy, and international reproducibility.

  • Tables: Tables should be self-explanatory and typed on separate sheets. Headings must be placed at the top separated by a horizontal line. Vertical lines are strictly prohibited in all tables. Sized to fit single column (83 mm) or full page width (176 mm).
  • Statistical Validity: Standard error of means (SEm±), Least Significant Difference (LSD), or Critical Difference (CD at P=0.05) must be clearly reported for all quantitative comparisons. Percentage data must indicate appropriate angular or arcsine transformations.
  • Figures & Illustrations: High-resolution graphs and photographs (minimum 300 DPI) in TIFF, JPG, or vector formats. Line drawings and bar charts should be cleanly prepared in MS Excel or graphic software.
  • Scientific Nomenclature: Scientific binomial names must be italicized with the authority citation given upon first mention (e.g., Helicoverpa armigera Hübner, Magnaporthe oryzae Couch & Kohn). Common names should follow standard entomological/pathological registries.
  • Units & Abbreviations: Use standard International System of Units (SI) throughout (e.g., g/ha, kg/ha, a.i./ha, mm, mL, °C, h, min). Non-standard abbreviations must be defined at first mention in the text.

4. References and Harvard Citation Style

References must be cited in the text using the author-year Harvard format (e.g., Singh, 2018; Sharma and Rao, 2021; or Patel et al., 2023 for three or more authors). The reference list at the end of the manuscript must be arranged alphabetically by first author surname.

Journal titles must be abbreviated strictly according to the World List of Scientific Periodicals or standard international indexing conventions.

Reference Formatting Examples

Journal Article:
Reddy, K. M., Prasad, R. D. and Sarma, B. K. 2022. Biological management of soil-borne pathogens in oilseed crops using native Trichoderma isolates. Indian Journal of Plant Protection, 50(2): 112–119.

Book:
Grafton-Cardwell, E. E. and Godfrey, L. D. 2018. Integrated Pest Management for Citrus (3rd ed.). University of California Agriculture and Natural Resources, Oakland, USA, 280 pp.

Edited Book Chapter:
Venkateswarlu, B. and Rao, C. S. 2016. Climate change and pest management in rainfed agriculture. In: Plant Protection in the 21st Century (Eds. P. K. Singh and A. K. Misra), Academic Publishers, New Delhi, pp. 45–68.

Conference Proceedings:
Kumar, P., Sridhar, V. and Sharma, K. 2021. Invasive pest surveillance and biosecurity protocols in South Asia. In: Proceedings of the National Symposium on Plant Health Management, Hyderabad, India, pp. 88–92.

5. Online Submission and Peer Review Workflow

Authors can submit their manuscripts through the official ICAR e-Publications national journal portal or email softcopies directly to the editorial desk. All submissions undergo double-blind peer review by designated subject matter referees.

  • Double-Blind Peer Review: Every article is evaluated anonymously by two independent peer reviewers and the Editorial Board for scientific novelty, methodology, and relevance.
  • Ethical Standards & Plagiarism: Authors must guarantee that the manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. Text similarity is verified via standard plagiarism detection systems.
  • Copyright: Upon acceptance of the manuscript, copyright is transferred to the Plant Protection Association of India.
  • Editorial Communication: All editorial queries and softcopy submissions can be directed to the Chief Editor at ijpp1972@yahoo.co.in.
