Author Guidelines

Scope & Membership Mandate

Publication in the Indian Journal of Plant Protection is open to members of the Plant Protection Association of India. All authors must ensure active membership of the society at the time of publication.

The journal considers original research papers on applied aspects of plant protection, including insect pests, plant diseases, nematodes, rodents, weeds, and plant protection equipment. Purely routine laboratory studies or single-season observations are discouraged; field trials on integrated pest management (IPM), crop loss assessment, and host plant resistance should ideally be based on a minimum of two seasons of experimentation.

  • Active Society Membership: All contributing authors must hold active membership in PPAI prior to publication.
  • Student Theses Routing: Research articles originating from Master’s or Ph.D. thesis work must be routed through the student’s research guide or a member of the Journal Advisory Board.
  • Processing Charges: Article processing charges are applicable with respect to all accepted articles as per society norms.

Manuscript Preparation & Formatting

Manuscripts should not exceed 6,000 words in total length (including abstract, references, and tables). The typescript must be prepared in double space on standard A-4 size paper with 2.5 cm margins on all sides.

Authors must strictly adhere to the journal’s typographical specifications:

  • Title and Main Headings: Set in 14 pt Arial Black for the title, and 12 pt Arial Black for all primary section headings.
  • Body Text: Set in 10 pt Times New Roman, double-spaced throughout.
  • Heading Hierarchy: Primary headings in 12 pt Arial Black; secondary headings in 10 pt bold; tertiary headings in 10 pt italics running directly into the text.
  • Language: Authors are advised to use clear, concise, and unambiguous English while preparing the manuscript.

Manuscript Structure & Organization

Each manuscript should be organized into clearly demarcated sections in the following sequence:

  • Title Page: A short and informative title with initial capitals for each word, together with full names, institutional affiliations, and email addresses of all authors. A running title of not more than 50 characters must be provided if the main title exceeds one line.
  • Abstract: A brief, informative abstract of 200 to 250 words stating the research problem, methodology, principal results, and practical conclusions.
  • Keywords: Four to six relevant keywords listed in alphabetical order immediately following the abstract.
  • Introduction: Brief statement of the problem, background literature, and specific objectives of the study.
  • Materials and Methods: Clear description of experimental design, location, environmental conditions, sampling protocols, treatments, and analytical methods.
  • Results and Discussion: Results and discussion should be integrated with supporting empirical data in tables or figures, interpreted in relation to relevant published work.

Tables, Figures & Scientific Standards

Tables should be self-explanatory and typed on separate sheets. Headings must be placed as part of the table, separated by a horizontal line. Vertical lines in tables are strictly prohibited. Tables should be sized to fit either a single column (83 mm) or the full page width (176 mm).

Statistical tests, standard error of the mean (SEm±), Least Significant Difference (LSD), or Critical Difference (CD at P=0.05) must be clearly stated for all quantitative data.

  • Scientific Nomenclature: Scientific binomial names must be italicized with author citation given at first mention in the text (e.g., Helicoverpa armigera Hübner).
  • Standard SI Units: Use standard metric and SI units throughout (g/ha, kg/ha, a.i./ha, mm, mL, °C, min, h). Abbreviations must be defined upon first occurrence.
  • Figures and Illustrations: Graphs should be cleanly prepared in MS Excel or graphic software; photographs should be high-resolution (minimum 300 DPI).

References & Citation Protocol

References must be cited in the text following the Harvard author-year format (e.g., Singh, 2018; Sharma and Rao, 2021; or Patel et al., 2023 for three or more authors). The reference list at the end of the manuscript must be arranged alphabetically by the first author’s surname without numbering.

Journal titles must be abbreviated strictly according to the World List of Scientific Periodicals.

  • Journal Article: Reddy, K. M., Prasad, R. D. and Sarma, B. K. 2022. Biological management of soil-borne pathogens in oilseed crops using native Trichoderma isolates. Indian Journal of Plant Protection, 50(2): 112–119.
  • Book: Grafton-Cardwell, E. E. and Godfrey, L. D. 2018. Integrated Pest Management for Citrus (3rd ed.). University of California Agriculture and Natural Resources, Oakland, USA, 280 pp.
  • Book Chapter: Venkateswarlu, B. and Rao, C. S. 2016. Climate change and pest management in rainfed agriculture. In: Plant Protection in the 21st Century (Eds. P. K. Singh and A. K. Misra), Academic Publishers, New Delhi, pp. 45–68.

Submission & Peer Review Process

Manuscripts should be submitted online through the ICAR e-Publications national journal portal (https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJPP) or sent as a softcopy via email to the Chief Editor at ijpp1972@yahoo.co.in. Physical hard copies are not accepted.

All submitted manuscripts undergo a double-blind peer review process by subject specialists. Upon final acceptance, copyright is transferred to the Plant Protection Association of India.
